## Executive Summary

Debridgers is a Kaduna-based agricultural produce and food distribution company that sources staple commodities from suppliers and farmers and delivers them to business and institutional buyers. Since launch, we have built and proven a working procurement-to-delivery system: a verified digital payment and reconciliation platform, a corrected and tested pricing model, and a small but genuine base of repeat business customers, all served from Kaduna.

Following a recent meeting with the Director-General, we identified two areas where a structured partnership with KADA could convert an existing relationship into measurable commercial and developmental impact: first, supplying raw materials for compound feed production to a buyer identified through KADA's network, and second, running a pilot input-credit ("outgrower") programme with smallholder maize and soya bean farmers, in which farmers receive inputs ahead of planting in exchange for an agreed quantity of produce at harvest.

Neither proposal asks KADA for capital. What we are requesting is confirmation of specific details, a short joint working session to design the outgrower pilot's pricing structure, guidance on where to concentrate the farmer pilot, and KADA's endorsement of the resulting agreement with farmers - the kind of institutional credibility an early-stage private company cannot offer on its own. This note sets out our background, the problem as we understand it, what we are proposing, how we intend to run it, and what we would consider success.

## 1. Background and Organisational Capacity

Debridgers LTD is registered with the Corporate Affairs Commission (RC Number **9573579**), holds a **Nigeria Revenue Service Tax Identification** Number, and is registered with the Special Control Unit Against Money Laundering (SCUML) under the EFCC. We operate a business-to-business agricultural produce and food distribution model in Kaduna. Our current operation sources staple commodities - rice, beans, and cooking oil - into a small warehouse and delivers them to food-service businesses on a prepaid basis, meaning customers pay in advance of delivery. This has allowed the company to grow without needing to hold financed inventory: to date, every recorded order has been paid for before goods left the warehouse.

The company has processed more than ten orders across two repeat business customers, acquired entirely through direct outreach by the founding team, and has built a payment and reconciliation system that tracks every transaction to the naira and kobo. That system has been independently tested through more than seventy automated checks covering payment confirmation, wallet accuracy, and account isolation, and has completed live transactions through our payment processor without incident.

We are a founder-led team of four: two technical co-founders who jointly cover backend engineering, infrastructure, and business development; a mobile developer who also leads sales outreach; and a designer who leads brand and campaign material. All four currently also work directly in the field, since the company has not yet engaged external sales agents.

We describe our purpose this way: **Debridgers replaces opaque, uncoordinated intermediation with transparent, measurable, and lower-cost distribution.** We do not claim to eliminate the intermediaries who already serve farmers and buyers well - aggregators, transporters, financiers, and graders all provide real value. Our claim is that the chain connecting farmer to buyer can be made more transparent and better coordinated, and that this is worth building toward deliberately rather than leaving to chance.

## 2. Statement of Need

Nigerian agriculture is widely understood to suffer from a disconnect between farmers, who often lack organised and predictable access to markets, and buyers, who often cannot source the volume, quality, and consistency of supply they need from any single channel. This is the general problem that businesses like Debridgers exist to address.

Our recent conversation with KADA's Director-General sharpened our understanding of where Debridgers specifically can add value. The Director-General's assessment was direct: sourcing produce is not expected to be Debridgers' constraint, given the scale and organisation of farmer engagement that KADA already maintains across the state. The constraint that determines whether a business like ours succeeds is instead on the demand and execution side - structuring reliable buyers, managing working capital, handling storage, and running offtake arrangements with enough discipline that the supply already available through the state's agricultural network can actually reach a paying buyer in usable form.

This understanding shapes what we are proposing. We are not asking KADA to help us find farmers in general terms, and we are not proposing to duplicate work the agency already does. We are proposing two specific, boundable pieces of work that use KADA's existing reach and credibility against the gap Debridgers genuinely has: converting available supply into structured, reliable commercial outcomes.

## 3. Proposed Partnership

We are proposing two related but independently workable initiatives.

### 3.1 Feed Raw Materials Supply

The Director-General identified a buyer - referred to in our discussion as the "station market" - requiring a range of raw materials used in compound feed production: maize, groundnut, groundnut cake, wheat offal, maize offal, palm kernel cake, methionine, lysine, and salt. This represents the majority of the standard raw-material list used in feed formulation.

Debridgers is well-positioned to source maize and groundnut directly, and is prepared to establish supplier relationships for the remaining materials as required. Before committing to a supply volume, we would need KADA's assistance confirming the buyer's exact requirements - quantities, delivery frequency, and quality specification - so that we can price and commit to this relationship on a sound footing rather than a provisional one. Once confirmed, we propose to run an initial supply order to prove out the logistics and payment arrangement, followed by a recurring supply relationship.

### 3.2 Outgrower and Input-Credit Pilot

We propose a structured pilot in which Debridgers distributes farm inputs - fertiliser, and where appropriate herbicide and pesticide - to a defined group of smallholder farmers ahead of planting, under a signed agreement, in exchange for an agreed quantity of produce delivered back at harvest. Debridgers would monitor participating farms through the growing season and operate an aggregation point at harvest to collect the produce owed under each agreement. The scale discussed in our initial conversation was in the order of 100 soya bean farmers and 200 maize farmers, concentrated in Local Government Areas to be identified jointly with KADA, based on the agency's existing knowledge of farmer concentration and readiness.

We consider it important to design this pilot's pricing structure carefully before it begins, rather than treat it as a simple exchange of inputs for a fixed quantity of produce. Commodity prices between the time inputs are distributed and the time of harvest can move substantially, and a scheme that fixes only the quantity owed - without any protection on price - places the full risk of an adverse price movement on whoever supplied the inputs. We would welcome the opportunity to design this element of the pilot jointly with KADA, drawing on the agency's experience with similar programmes, before any agreement is signed with farmers.

We understand that KADA already maintains weekly reporting on farm produce and the prices it is achieving at market. We see this as a significant asset to the pilot rather than a gap we need to fill: it means the season-level monitoring this pilot needs already exists, and, more importantly, it gives us a realistic basis for a price-risk trigger - a way of recognising an adverse price movement while the season is still under way, rather than discovering it only at harvest. We would welcome KADA's guidance on how best to make use of this existing reporting for the pilot's target Local Government Areas.

We are requesting KADA's partnership on farmer access and, in particular, KADA's willingness to co-sign or formally endorse the resulting farmer agreement. An agreement carrying the agency's institutional backing will be substantially more credible to farmers than one from a company they have no prior relationship with, and this endorsement is, in our view, the single most valuable form of support KADA could offer this pilot.

Neither initiative in this note is a request for capital or an equity partnership. What we are asking for is confirmation of specific details, collaborative design work on the pilot's structure, and KADA's institutional support - set out fully in section 6.

## 4. Objectives

1. Establish Debridgers as a reliable, traceable supplier of raw materials to the identified feed-production buyer, within an initial supply agreement scoped jointly with KADA.
2. Design and launch a pilot outgrower programme covering a defined, modest number of maize and soya bean farmers, with a pricing structure that protects both the farmer and Debridgers from an adverse price movement between planting and harvest.
3. Demonstrate, through the pilot, whether a wider rollout of the outgrower model - toward the fuller scale originally discussed - is commercially and operationally sound before committing further resources to it.
4. Build a documented, repeatable working relationship between Debridgers and KADA that can extend to further commodities or geographies once the pilot has proven itself.

---

## 5. Implementation Approach and Timeline

We propose a phased approach, with firm dates to be agreed once the specific details in section 6 are confirmed:

**Phase one - design**: Confirm the feed buyer's required volumes and pricing; design the outgrower pilot's pricing-risk structure jointly with KADA; select target Local Government Areas; draft the outgrower agreement for KADA's review and endorsement.

**Phase two - pilot**: Fulfil an initial feed raw-materials order to prove the supply relationship; sign outgrower agreements with a modest first cohort of farmers, held below the fuller 100/200 figures originally discussed until the pricing structure has been tested against a real season.

**Phase three - harvest and settlement**: Operate the aggregation point at harvest; collect produce against each farmer's agreement; settle accounts; record the pilot's actual results in detail.

**Phase four - review**: Assess the pilot against clear, pre-agreed criteria - what worked, what the real price exposure looked like, and whether the model is ready to scale toward the fuller farmer numbers - before any decision to expand.

A longer-term view of Debridgers' growth, extending toward regional distribution infrastructure over the coming years, exists in our internal planning but is deliberately not detailed here; this note is scoped to what can realistically be committed to in the near term.

---

## 6. What We Are Requesting from KADA

1. **Confirmation of the feed raw-materials buyer's identity and required volumes**, so that supply commitments can be priced and made with confidence rather than provisionally.
2. **A joint working session to design the outgrower pilot's pricing-risk structure** - for example, a floor price, a standing arrangement with a processor as a buyer of last resort in the event of a price collapse, or an input-to-output ratio that already accounts for normal price volatility - before any agreement is signed with farmers.
3. **Guidance on which Local Government Areas to target** for the farmer pilot, drawing on KADA's existing knowledge of farmer concentration and readiness.
4. **KADA's in-principle willingness to co-sign or formally endorse the outgrower agreement**, which we consider essential to the pilot's credibility with farmers who do not yet have a relationship with Debridgers.
5. **Access to KADA's existing weekly farm-produce and market-price reporting for the pilot Local Government Areas**, so the pilot's monitoring and its price-risk trigger can be built on data that already exists.

---

## 7. Expected Outcomes

For the **farmers** participating in the outgrower pilot: guaranteed access to farm inputs ahead of planting without an upfront cash requirement, and a guaranteed buyer for the agreed produce at harvest - addressing two of the most common causes of a difficult season for a smallholder farmer.

For the **feed buyer**: a second, traceable, well-documented supplier of raw materials.

For **KADA**: a measurable, carefully documented pilot of a farmer-support model that the agency can evaluate for wider application, without carrying the day-to-day operational or financial burden of running it directly.

For **Debridgers**: a second commercial relationship distinct from our existing food-business customer base, and direct operational experience running the farmer-facing side of our intended long-term model.

---

## 8. Monitoring, Risk, and Accountability

We intend to track, at minimum: the volume and reliability of feed-material deliveries against the buyer's requirements; the proportion of enrolled farmers who complete the season under agreement; the actual input cost versus produce value realised per farmer at settlement, measured against the pricing-risk structure agreed in the design phase; and the throughput of the harvest aggregation point. Season-level monitoring itself will draw on KADA's existing weekly produce and price reporting rather than a parallel system built for this pilot alone.

We have identified the following risks candidly, because we believe a partner should see them stated rather than discover them later: the price-risk exposure inherent in any input-for-produce exchange, discussed above; the possibility of partial delivery or default by individual farmers, for which the agreement will need a clear, fair treatment; the risk of committing supplier relationships against a buyer volume that is not yet fully confirmed; and the operational capacity of a small founding team already running an existing business alongside this pilot. None of these are reasons to avoid the pilot - they are the reasons we propose starting at a scale small enough to absorb a wrong assumption before scaling further.

## 9. Conclusion

Debridgers has built a working, tested, and honestly-assessed distribution business at small scale in Kaduna. The conversation with KADA's Director-General identified a clear and specific way to grow that business in a direction that also serves farmers and the state's broader agricultural development goals, rather than growth for its own sake. We are not asking KADA for money. We are asking for the specific information, design collaboration, and institutional endorsement that would let us run a carefully bounded pilot, measure it honestly, and report back before either side commits to anything larger.

We would welcome the opportunity to discuss this note further at KADA's convenience.
