## 1. Position

Debridgers should not present itself as a grocery startup seeking investors. It should present itself as:

> **A technology-enabled food distribution business proving a repeatable, transparent, capital-efficient model in Kaduna.**

The competitive advantage is not the software. It is the possible combination of direct supply, recurring B2B demand, a trusted local distribution layer, efficient delivery, transparent prices, and transaction data.

**The immediate mission is not to become national. It is to make Kaduna economically undeniable.**

## 2. What changed since the last strategy

Three findings reshape everything downstream, and any conversation using the older framing will be wrong.

**The margin was never thin; the price list was wrong.** Earlier analysis reasoned from a ₦1,400 unit price that came from a hardcoded placeholder. Real packages are ₦12,000 to ₦55,000. Gross margin is ₦2,500 to ₦3,300 per package at a 6% spread. What was broken was a ₦100 handling fee and a ₦500 delivery fee against a **measured** ₦4,000 trip cost.

Break-even procurement spread on a single bag of rice: **10.57% under current live pricing, -0.36% under the locked pricing.** A negative figure means the order clears even at full market price. The repricing does not merely reduce the requirement to be an exceptional buyer; it removes it, and turns procurement spread into upside.

**Working capital is negative.** Customers pay before delivery and only confirmed orders are processed. The buyer finances the trade. This is the strongest structural fact about the business and it changes what capital is for.

**Demand is proven at two customers.** Ten-plus orders, both reordering, all from founder outreach. Small, real, and concentrated.

## 5. Capital strategy

### What capital is not for

**Debridgers does not need capital to buy stock.** Customers pay first. The ₦300,000 buffer covers settlement gaps, up-front transport, and replacements. Saying "we need money for inventory" invites the question of why the model consumes cash, and the honest answer is that it does not.

### What capital is for

**Serving buyers who will not prepay.** Institutional buyers, established restaurant groups, and anything with a procurement department pay on delivery or on terms as policy, not distrust. Under prepayment-only they cannot be served. The company is not turning down orders for lack of demand; it is turning them down because the balance sheet cannot carry them.

```text
Credit float required = monthly GMV on terms × (days of terms ÷ 30)
```

| Accounts on terms | Monthly GMV | Float at 14 days | Float at 30 days |
| ----------------- | ----------- | ---------------- | ---------------- |
| 5                 | ₦1,605,000  | ₦749,000         | ₦1,605,000       |
| 10                | ₦3,210,000  | ₦1,498,000       | ₦3,210,000       |
| 20                | ₦6,420,000  | ₦2,996,000       | ₦6,420,000       |

**The float revolves, it does not burn.** That is why it suits debt, an overdraft, invoice financing, or an agricultural facility. **Do not sell equity to fund a revolving float.**

Three rules travel with any terms offering, or the advantage becomes a liability:

1. **Price the terms.** A buyer on 30-day terms is borrowing for a month. That should cost them, as a higher rate or a forgone prepayment discount. Free credit is an unrecorded discount
2. **Provision for bad debt** from the first terms account, before the first default
3. **Earn terms, do not grant them.** Prepay for the first three orders, terms afterwards on request against a per-account credit limit. The order history is the credit file, and it is the same asset a lender will underwrite against

### Sequence

| Phase                                  | Trigger                                                            | Instrument                                                        |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Now**                                | Nothing proven beyond two customers                                | Founder capital only. Measure and sell                            |
| **After measurement and 12+ accounts** | Contribution margin measured positive, order growth predictable    | Grants and non-dilutive first. Small revolving facility for terms |
| **After predictable B2B cash flow**    | 3+ months of stable contribution profit, documented supplier depth | Institutional pre-seed, if the ambition requires it               |

**Debt before predictable cash flow magnifies mistakes.** A revolving facility against proven order history is a different instrument from a loan against a plan.

## 6. Partner strategy

### The discipline point

> A signed MoU with no transactions is not traction.

Every partnership is scored in one of six units and nothing else: **revenue, orders, supply secured, financing unlocked, customers reached, cost removed.** A partnership that cannot be scored in one of those does not go on the list.

### Buyer-access partners

The question is not "how do we advertise to Kaduna." It is **"who already has 100, 500, or 5,000 people who need food."** Concentrated demand at a single delivery point is the best available answer to a delivery cost problem, because the trip is the cost.

| Channel                                      | Offer                                                                               | Why they say yes                                                 | Pilot shape                                  |
| -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| **Restaurants and caterers**                 | Fixed weekly pricing on rice, beans, oil. Scheduled delivery. Replacement guarantee | Procurement predictability, no staff sent to market, no haggling | 3 accounts, weekly standing order, 8 weeks   |
| **Hotels**                                   | Same, at contract rates above 20 packages                                           | Volume pricing plus reliability                                  | 1 account, monthly order                     |
| **Large employers**                          | Staff food programme at market price, delivered to premises                         | Staff benefit at no margin cost to the employer                  | 1 employer, monthly cycle, single drop point |
| **Universities**                             | Catering and hostel procurement                                                     | Budget predictability and transparent pricing                    | 1 institution, termly contract               |
| **Residential communities and associations** | Group buying at a single drop point                                                 | Members pay market price without the trip                        | 1 estate, monthly cycle                      |
| **Supermarkets**                             | Wholesale supply of hero products                                                   | Reliable staple supply without their own sourcing                | Later. Requires supplier depth first         |

**The economics behind this list.** Contribution rises with basket size until the caps bite, so the sweet spot is **3 to 20 packages, roughly ₦100,000 to ₦750,000 per order.** Employers and institutions deliver that in one drop. That is the arithmetic reason for the segment order, not a preference.

### Supply partners

The goal is **a small number of reliable suppliers, not a large farmer database.** What matters: reliable supply, known quality, predictable price, predictable quantity, predictable replenishment, transparent records.

| Partner                                          | Debridgers wants                                               | Debridgers offers                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Farmer cooperatives**                          | Reliable supply, known quality, predictable price and quantity | Guaranteed off-take, transparent pricing, payment records, demand visibility |
| **KADA**, Kaduna Agricultural Development Agency | Farmer and cooperative introductions, local credibility        | Market linkage for state farmers, digital transaction records, demand data   |
| **Agricultural research institutions**           | Quality benchmarks, variety guidance                           | A distribution channel for improved output                                   |
| **Existing suppliers and aggregators**           | Continuity while farmer relationships are built                | Repeat volume and reliable payment                                           |

**Do not treat existing intermediaries as adversaries.** Debridgers works with suppliers who are intermediaries and will continue to where they supply quality. The thesis is transparency and coordination, not elimination.

### Logistics partners

Delivery is the second-largest cost and the one with a measurement behind it. A logistics relationship is worth pursuing only once volumes are predictable enough to be worth a partner's routing.

| Partner                         | Debridgers offers                       | Pilot metric                                    |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Local transport owners**      | Consistent weekly volume in fixed zones | Cost per drop below the zone base fee           |
| **GIG Logistics or equivalent** | Predictable regional freight, later     | Cost per package below the current tapered rate |
| **ColdHubs**                    | Volume, much later                      | Not applicable until perishables are in range   |

**Two rules about this list.** Order count is a vanity metric at a ₦35,000 median package; **AOV and contribution are where the business moves.** And **twenty inactive agents are worse than five productive ones**, so agent count is never a target.

## 10. What to stop doing

1. **Stop building features without a commercial reason.** The halt is a decision, not a mood
2. **Stop considering expansion before density.** A second city means new suppliers, agents, logistics, acquisition, and complexity, all at once
3. **Stop discussing fundraising before contribution margin is measured.** The first question will be whether an additional order creates or destroys value
4. **Stop optimising for agent count**
5. **Stop treating partnerships as vanity metrics.** Score them in the six units
6. **Stop subsidising demand blindly.** The ₦500 referral discount and free delivery promotions are unpriced against incremental orders, repeat behaviour, and contribution
7. **Stop taking debt before cash flow is predictable**
8. **Stop selling on the old price list.** Done: the corrected pricing is shipped in code and in the database. The old list required a 10.6% procurement advantage to break even

## 12. Operating principles

1. **Measure before you scale.** One measurement invalidated a ₦2,500 estimate and moved every number in the model. Nine cost lines remain unmeasured
2. **Density before geography.** Become the easiest and most trusted way to buy rice, beans, and oil in a small number of dense areas
3. **Margin comes from procurement.** Fees recover cost; they do not profit
4. **Charge what the trip costs.** Delivery is priced from measurement, not from what feels saleable
5. **Never pay an agent a percentage of order value.** Naira per package, verifiable by counting
6. **Earn exposure, do not grant it.** Order-first for agents, prepayment-first for buyers
7. **A partnership without transactions is not traction**
8. **Write decisions down and date them.** Three documents carried a ₦1,400 placeholder for months because nobody wrote down what the company actually sells. **Now in practice:** every locked decision carries its date, the shipped work is dated in the migrations that applied it, and `packages/pricing/src/assumptions.ts` records each business figure with a status of measured, estimated or unknown so a placeholder can never again read as a fact

## 13. Three-year path

**Year 1: win Kaduna.** Measured economics, 12+ repeat B2B accounts, ₦3m+ monthly GMV, supplier depth on three hero products, the first order-first agents, and monthly contribution profit covering all fixed costs including imputed rent and market salaries.

**Year 2: regional density.** A second city only against the written trigger. Agent network at meaningful scale. Institutional and employer contracts recurring. A revolving facility in place, underwritten by order history.

**Year 3: regional platform.** Multiple cities with local supplier depth, B2B procurement as the revenue core, farmer aggregation beginning, price intelligence becoming a real dataset, and logistics orchestrated rather than hired ad hoc.

**The long-term moat** is not the technology. It is **dense supply plus dense demand plus trusted agents plus transaction data plus price history plus financial relationships plus operational knowledge.** Replacing Debridgers would mean replacing all of them at once.
