## THE THESIS

Earlier framing was "middlemen are the problem." That has been retired, because Debridgers already works with suppliers who are intermediaries and will continue to where they supply quality. Some intermediaries provide real services: aggregation, transport, storage, financing, grading, risk absorption, market discovery.

The current, defensible thesis:

> **Debridgers replaces opaque, uncoordinated intermediation with transparent, measurable, and lower-cost distribution.**

Public expression: **"Market Prices. Zero Market Stress."**

---

## THE PROBLEM

**For businesses.** Restaurants, caterers, and food businesses need consistent volume at predictable prices. Today that means renegotiating constantly, absorbing price volatility, sending staff to market, and having no supply guarantee.

**For households.** Market prices are opaque and change without explanation. Going to Central Market costs time and transport, and the price paid depends on the buyer's ability to haggle. Buying closer to home means paying retail markup.

**For farmers.** No direct access to end markets. They sell to whoever arrives at the farm gate, with no visibility into what the produce fetches downstream, no reliable bulk off-take, and no demand signal to plan against.

**For the ecosystem.** There is no public reference price, so nobody can tell an exploitative price from a fair one.

## HOW THE BUSINESS WORKS TODAY

```text
Supplier  ->  Debridgers  ->  B2B buyer
              (warehouse,
               founder-run
               delivery)
```

1. **Procurement.** Debridgers sources staples from suppliers into warehouse inventory. Running and proven
2. **Demand.** Founders prospect food businesses by WhatsApp, phone call, and personal visit. This produced every order to date
3. **Payment.** The customer pays before delivery, by card, bank transfer, or a dedicated virtual account. Confirmed orders only
4. **Fulfilment.** Stock is dispatched from the warehouse and delivered by a hired vehicle, priced by zone
5. **Reconciliation.** Every naira is tracked in integer kobo and reconciled against Paystack

### The target chain

```text
Farmer  ->  Debridgers  ->  Buyers
            (warehouse +
             thin delivery
             layer)
```

Moving from suppliers to farmers is an operational upgrade to be earned, not a simplification. A supplier is one phone call with known quality and immediate availability. A farmer is a relationship, a season, a quality risk, and a lead time.

## TRADING POSITION: DISTRIBUTOR, NOT PACKAGER

**Confirmed.** Debridgers sells packages exactly as received, with the supplier's seal intact. It does not open, re-portion, weigh, or re-bag.

This keeps packaging cost at zero, keeps the company on the distributor side of NAFDAC's line, and keeps the quality guarantee cheap to honour, because an unopened seal is evidence that the contents are what the supplier sent.

**The company intends to become a packager.** That is a scheduled change, not a hypothetical, and it adds packaging materials, a calibrated scale, fill labour, labels, and grain shrinkage as costs, moves the NAFDAC obligation, and transfers quality risk from the supplier to Debridgers. **Obtain a compliance opinion before the first bag is opened.**

## VALUE PROPOSITIONS

**To buyers.** Market price at the door, already delivered. Quality guaranteed with no-questions replacement, already honoured. Fixed weekly prices with no haggling. Order by WhatsApp, phone, or web. Fees stated up front and calculated identically at quote and checkout.

The strongest differentiator is **price certainty plus a guaranteed replacement.** A restaurant that knows what a bag of rice costs on Monday, gets it delivered, and knows a bad bag will be replaced without argument, has three things the incumbent market does not offer together.

**To agents.** Stock without capital, weekly bank payouts, own neighbourhood, free training, a growth path through recruitment overrides and a state manager tier. Nobody is receiving this yet.

**To suppliers and farmers.** Reliable repeat off-take, payment on agreed terms with records, direct market access without an intermediary spread. Farmer-facing software does not exist.

## PRICING MODEL

Goods are sold at market reference price. **Margin comes from buying better, not charging more.**

| Charge                | Rule                                                             | Purpose                                                                   |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Goods**             | Market reference price                                           | The entire profit engine. Target procurement spread 6% to 10%             |
| **Delivery**          | Zone base covering 2 packages, then a taper, with a per-drop cap | Cost recovery, set to the measured trip cost                              |
| **Cost-to-serve fee** | 3% of items subtotal, floor ₦500, cap ₦5,000                     | Payment rail, order admin, reconciliation, support, replacement provision |
| **Minimum order**     | ₦25,000 or 2 packages                                            | A solvency rule. Below it no fee structure covers the trip                |

**Delivery table:**

| Zone         | Base, 2 packages | Packages 3 to 6 | Packages 7+ | Cap per drop |
| ------------ | ---------------- | --------------- | ----------- | ------------ |
| Kaduna South | ₦4,000           | ₦700 each       | ₦400 each   | ₦10,000      |
| Kaduna North | ₦4,500           | ₦800 each       | ₦450 each   | ₦11,000      |
| Chikun       | ₦6,000           | ₦1,000 each     | ₦600 each   | ₦14,000      |

**Above 20 packages or ₦750,000 of goods, quote individually:** delivery at actual cost plus 20%, cost-to-serve negotiated.

**This pricing shipped on 2026-08-29**, in code and in the database. It replaced a ₦100 flat handling fee and a ₦500 zone base that lost money on every small order. The taper and the per-drop cap are columns on the zone table, so a far zone is priced as a far zone. Rules live in `packages/pricing`, the single source both the API and the browser read. Full derivation in `BusinessModel.md`.

## WORKING CAPITAL

**Customers pay before delivery, and only confirmed orders are processed.** The buyer finances the trade. This is a negative working capital cycle and it is the most attractive structural fact about the business.

The ₦300,000 is not order-size capital. It is a buffer for settlement gaps, up-front transport, replacements, and cancellations.

**The constraint it creates:** buyers who require payment on delivery or on terms, which includes most institutional buyers, cannot be served. Capital converts that from a refusal into a priced product.

```text
Credit float required = monthly GMV on terms × (days of terms ÷ 30)
```

| Accounts on terms | Monthly GMV | Float at 14 days | Float at 30 days |
| ----------------- | ----------- | ---------------- | ---------------- |
| 5                 | ₦1,605,000  | ₦749,000         | ₦1,605,000       |
| 10                | ₦3,210,000  | ₦1,498,000       | ₦3,210,000       |
| 20                | ₦6,420,000  | ₦2,996,000       | ₦6,420,000       |

The float revolves rather than burns, which suits debt, an overdraft, invoice financing, or a NIRSAL or Bank of Agriculture facility better than equity.

## AGENT MODEL

No agents recruited. The four founders perform the role. The platform machinery is built and unused.

**Commission, locked:** flat naira per package, never a percentage.

| Band              | Products                 | Commission |
| ----------------- | ------------------------ | ---------- |
| Beans, 100kg bag  | Wake Gida, Cowpea        | ₦1,200     |
| Rice, 50kg bag    | Local White, Tuwo, Ofada | ₦1,000     |
| Oil, 25 litre keg | Palm Oil, Groundnut Oil  | ₦700       |
| Non-hero          | Garri, Yam, Irish Potato | ₦400       |

Rule: commission stays between **30% and 45% of gross margin**. At the assumed 6% spread these sit at 36%, 40%, and 42%. **At a 3% real spread they are unaffordable and the level must drop.**

**Consignment exposure, locked.** A real package is ₦27,000 to ₦54,000 of company stock in a stranger's hands.

| Limit                                         | Value                                |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Total outstanding, all agents                 | ≤ 30% of cash on hand, ₦90,000 today |
| Per agent, first 8 weeks                      | ₦50,000                              |
| Per agent, after 8 weeks above 95% remittance | ₦150,000                             |
| Maximum days outstanding                      | 7 days                               |

**Policy: new agents start order-first.** The agent takes a paid order, then Debridgers releases stock, then the agent delivers. Zero exposure. Consignment is earned, not granted.

**Remittance metrics, defined so the earlier confusion cannot recur:**

| Metric                | Definition                                        | Target   |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| Remittance rate       | Value remitted ÷ value due                        | > 95%    |
| Days outstanding      | Mean age of unremitted stock value                | < 7 days |
| Default rate          | Value written off ÷ value issued                  | < 2%     |
| Agent activation rate | Agents with a completed order ÷ registered agents | > 70%    |

The previously recorded "agent remittance rate: 5%" was undefined, not alarming: no agents existed, so no stock was issued and nothing was due. The figure most likely leaked from the commission setting. **Keeping commission in naira per package makes that confusion impossible to repeat.**

An agent order is worth roughly **₦1,151 less** than a direct one, ₦1,520 against ₦2,671 on a bag of rice, and costs no founder time and no vehicle. An agent sale collects no cost-to-serve fee and no delivery fee, which is where the gap comes from.

## WAREHOUSE

A co-founder's house. **₦20,000 per month is booked as an in-kind contribution by that co-founder**, pending two real quotes for a small lock-up store in the operating area.

| Monthly throughput | Warehouse cost per bag | As % of a ₦3,300 beans margin |
| ------------------ | ---------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| 10 bags            | ₦2,000                 | **61%**                       |
| 30 bags            | ₦667                   | 20%                           |
| 100 bags           | ₦200                   | 6%                            |
| 300 bags           | ₦67                    | 2%                            |

**Capacity is one tonne**, which is 10 bags of beans at 100kg or 20 bags of rice at 50kg.

That is _standing_ capacity, not monthly throughput, and the distinction is the whole argument. The table above reaches 100 and 300 bags a month against a store that holds 10 to 20 at once, which is only possible if stock turns rather than sits: 300 bags a month through a 10-bag store is a turn a day. Prepayment is what makes that achievable, because goods can be bought against an order rather than held on speculation.

So the binding constraint is dwell time, and one tonne is only a ceiling if stock stops moving.

**At current volume the warehouse consumes roughly 60% of the gross margin on every bag.** That is a throughput problem, not a rent problem. The lever is **dwell time, not rent**: because customers prepay, Debridgers can run close to a cross-dock. **Do not sign a lease before 100 bags a month.**

## GLOSSARY

Every term this document uses that carries a specific meaning, with the arithmetic where there is any. `BusinessModel.md` holds the full glossary, including agent and trading terms; this covers what appears here.

### Money in an order

**Package.** One unit as the supplier sealed it: a 50kg bag of rice, a 100kg bag of beans, a 25 litre keg of oil. **Not a weight.** Delivery is priced per package because packages are what fill a vehicle, and because a buyer can verify a count by looking.

**Items subtotal.** The goods alone, before any fee.

**Delivery fee.** What the buyer pays to move the order, priced to recover the trip and not to profit. Zone base covering two packages, then a per-package taper, then a per-zone cap.

**Cost-to-serve fee.** `items subtotal × 3%`, floored at ₦500 and capped at ₦5,000. Covers the payment rail plus order admin, reconciliation, support and replacements. **Once called the "handling fee", which is why it used to be ₦100 flat.**

**Order total.** `items subtotal + delivery fee + cost-to-serve fee`.

### Procurement and margin

**Procurement.** Buying the goods: sourcing a supplier, agreeing a price, and getting the package into the warehouse. **The profit engine**, because Debridgers sells at market reference price and therefore earns on what it pays, not on what it charges.

**Market reference price.** What the buyer would pay for the same goods at Central Market. Debridgers sells at this price as a promise, not a tactic.

**Procurement spread.** `(market reference price − landed cost) ÷ market reference price`. Assumed at 6%, **unmeasured**.

**Landed cost.** What a package costs by the time it is in the warehouse: supplier price **plus inbound haulage** plus handling. Not the supplier's price; using that instead overstates the spread.

**Gross margin.** `items subtotal − landed cost`. Roughly ₦2,500 to ₦3,300 per hero package at a 6% spread.

**Contribution margin.** What survives every variable cost of one specific order: landed goods, payment fees, vehicle, loading, agent commission, replacements, discounts. **The number that says whether one more order helps or hurts.**

### Trading metrics

**GMV, gross merchandise value.** `sum of items subtotal across paid orders in a period`. The value of goods sold before costs, fees, refunds or discounts. **Not revenue** - Debridgers never earns GMV, it earns the spread plus fees on it.

**AOV, average order value.** `GMV ÷ number of paid orders`. At a ₦35,000 median package, order count flatters and AOV is where the business actually moves.

**Take rate.** `(procurement spread + fees) ÷ GMV`. Revenue captured as a share of goods sold.

### Cash

**Float, or credit float.** Cash permanently tied up serving customers who pay on terms instead of in advance: `monthly GMV on terms × (days of terms ÷ 30)`. **It revolves rather than burns**, which is why it suits debt and not equity.

**Negative working capital cycle.** The customer pays before the supplier does, so the buyer finances the trade. The current structure, and the most attractive structural fact about this business.

**Burn.** Net cash consumed per month. **Runway** is `cash on hand ÷ monthly burn`. Both unmeasured, which is why there is no runway figure anywhere in this document.

**Buffer.** The ₦300,000 on hand. Under prepayment this is not order-size capital; it absorbs settlement gaps, up-front transport and replacements.

**Equity.** Ownership of the company, as a percentage split between founders. Distinct from funding: equity is who owns the business, funding is what it runs on. **Agreed and documented.** Selling equity to cover a revolving float is the classic error, because the float comes back and ownership does not.

### Operations

**Dwell time.** How long a package sits in the warehouse before delivery. **The lever on warehouse cost per unit** - rent is not the lever, throughput is.

**Cross-dock.** Goods bought against an order already placed and moved straight out, barely resting in the warehouse. What prepayment makes possible, and what keeps one tonne of capacity from binding.

**Distributor.** Sells packages exactly as received, supplier's seal intact. What Debridgers is today. **Packager** opens and re-portions into its own units, which moves the NAFDAC obligation and the quality risk onto Debridgers. That is the line this business has not crossed.

**Order source.** Who raised an order: `self_serve`, `assisted` or `agent`. Recorded on the order rather than inferred.

## COMPETITOR SET IN KADUNA

Item 9, to be filled in. **Record terms, not prices.**

Price is the trap: goods are sold at market reference, so this business will not win on price and a table of naira figures only confirms that. What decides whether a caterer switches is everything around the price.

**Credit is the column that matters most.** A market trader offering seven days' credit beats a 3% cost-to-serve fee on convenience every time. Debridgers' customers prepay, and that prepayment is the working-capital engine, so knowing what credit they are offered elsewhere is the single highest-value fact here. If credit is normal in Kaduna, it is a bigger threat to the model than anyone's price.
