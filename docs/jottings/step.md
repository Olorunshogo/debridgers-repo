1. COMPANY_PROFILE.md — the fact sheet

Job: One place for “what is Debridgers,” not strategy.

Company snapshot
• Debridgers LTD, Kaduna (focus Kaduna South), founded
2025, bootstrapped pre-seed
• Team of 4, unpaid, all acting as field agents (no
agents hired yet)
• Founders: Stephanie (backend) + Olorunshogo (CTO);
Henry (mobile/sales); Abdulmalik (design)
• Structural risk: BD and code both sit with the two
founders

Thesis (updated)
• Old: “remove middlemen” — retired
• Now: replace opaque, uncoordinated intermediation with
transparent, measurable, lower-cost distribution
• Tagline: “Market Prices. Zero Market Stress.”

How it works today

Supplier → Debridgers (warehouse + founder delivery) → B2B
buyer

Target later: farmer → Debridgers → buyers. Still a
distributor (seal intact), not a packager yet.

Commercial core
• Primary segment: B2B food businesses (proven).
Institutions next. B2C deferred. Agents built but
unused.
• Heroes: rice, beans, oil (prices listed; median package
~₦35k)
• Money model: sell at market reference; profit from
procurement spread (assumed 6%, unmeasured)
• Fees: delivery by zone + 3% cost-to-serve (floor ₦500 /
cap ₦5,000) + ₦25k min order
• Prepayment → negative working capital (buyer finances
the trade)

Ops / tech / traction
• Warehouse = co-founder house (~1 tonne); don’t lease
before ~100 bags/month
• Paystack verified; feature work halted except pricing
• Traction: 2 B2B, 10+ orders, all from founder outreach;
GMV/AOV/burn unmeasured

Locked seven decisions + 12-month goals + expansion trigger
(Kaduna must pay its own fixed costs for 3 months before a
second city)

Open items that matter: landed cost (biggest), haulage,
burn, admin minutes, GMV/AOV, competitor credit terms in
Kaduna

2. BusinessModel.md — the money bible (~1,500 lines)

Job: Why every rate, fee, and decision exists.
Authoritative on numbers.

How to read it
Every claim is tagged: PROVEN / BUILT / CLAIMED / ASSUMED /
UNKNOWN.

Big correction
Earlier docs reasoned from a ₦1,400 placeholder. Real
packages are ₦12k–₦55k. Old fees (₦100 handling, ₦500
delivery) lost money vs a measured ₦4,000 trip. That drove
the lock of new pricing.

Nine canvas blocks

1. Segments — B2B first, institutions next
2. Value props — buyers / agents / suppliers
3. Channels — outreach proven; app unused as acquisition
4. Relationships — founder-led, named accounts
5. Revenue — procurement spread = profit; fees = cost
   recovery
6. Resources — team, house warehouse, ₦300k, platform
7. Activities — procure, price, sell, fulfill, reconcile
8. Partnerships — suppliers, KADA, logistics, finance
9. Costs — COGS ~90–95%, delivery, Paystack, loading,
   etc.

Heart of the doc: worked examples A–F (one keg →
institutional 35 packages), break-even procurement spreads,
and levers: trip density, basket size, procurement upside.
Also warehouse math, credit-float formula, agent
commission (naira/package) and exposure caps.

Seven locked decisions (full reasoning lives here)
Then: what still can’t be locked (landed cost), first 30
days, full glossary.

If Profile and Model disagree on a figure, trust the Model
(then check packages/pricing for what actually ships).

3. Company_Diagnosis.md — the executive verdict (~286
   lines)

Job: What was wrong, what got fixed, what to do next.
Investor-facing judgment.

Central conclusion (revised)
│ Had a pricing problem → solved and shipped 2026-08-29.
What’s left: measurement, then customer count.

What it argues
• Old “structurally too thin” conclusion was wrong
(₦1,400 fossil)
• Real margin at 6% spread: ~₦2,500–₦3,300 per package
• Old fees needed ~10.6% below market just to break even
on one bag of rice; locked pricing needs ~0–1%
• Demand, supply (supplier tier), negative WC, verified
payments, seven decisions = solid base

Bottlenecks in order

1. Unmeasured landed cost (critical; gates agent
   commission too)
2. Everything else unmeasured (only delivery is measured)

3. Pricing shipped (closed)
4. 2-customer concentration
5. Founders own both BD and code
6. Consignment risk designed out (order-first)
7. Wallet / NAFDAC / NDPR scoped

Investor like / dislike lists, then “what capital is for”:
not inventory (prepay); credit float for buyers who won’t
prepay → debt/revolving facility, not equity for float.

Focus order: measure → ship remaining defects → sell to
12–15 accounts → deepen supply → legal opinions → agents →
capital → second city only against the trigger.

One-liner: not strategy/feature/round that’s missing — ~2
days of counting + 90 days of selling.

4. Growth / investor / partnership strategy (~353 lines)

Job: How to raise, partner, and operate for 90 days / 3
years. Assumes the seven decisions.

Positioning
Not “grocery startup seeking investors.”
→ Technology-enabled food distribution proving a
capital-efficient model in Kaduna.
Mission: make Kaduna economically undeniable first.

Priority ladder (don’t skip)
Measure → pricing/defects → 12+ B2B → supplier depth → more
delivery data → compliance → agents → partnerships →
non-dilutive / revolving facility → equity → expansion.

Capital
• Not for stock
• For terms buyers (float formula + tables)
• Sequence: founder capital now → grants / small facility
after measurement + 12 accounts → institutional equity
only after stable contribution

Partners (scored only as revenue / orders / supply /
financing / customers / cost removed)
• Buyer access: restaurants, hotels, employers,
universities, estates
• Supply: coops, KADA, existing aggregators (not enemies)

• Logistics / Paystack / payments lawyer /
NIRSAL–BOA–NADF
• Approach order: lawyer & NAFDAC first (lead times),
then suppliers, then sell harder, capital last

90-day plan
• Days 1–30: truth (supplier register, GMV query,
transport/minute logs, customer interviews,
contribution sheet)
• Days 31–60: sell (12–15 named accounts, sales
artefacts, KADA, 3+ suppliers/hero)
• Days 61–90: standing orders, one institutional pilot,
data room, then capital talks

Also: weekly metrics, “stop doing” list, risk table,
operating principles, 3-year path, relationship map. Same
closing line as Diagnosis: counting first, then selling.
