# Debridgers - Executive Diagnosis

## Executive Diagnosis

My central conclusion is:

> **Debridgers does not primarily have a funding problem yet. It has a commercial-validation and unit-economics problem.**

That distinction matters enormously.

You have already built a surprisingly sophisticated technical system for an early company: payments, wallets, agent KYC, consignment stock, commissions, referral attribution, order lifecycle, reconciliation logic and an automated test suite.

But commercially, the evidence is still extremely thin:

- **10 orders**
- unknown GMV
- unknown revenue
- unknown AOV
- unknown repeat rate
- unknown CAC
- unknown supplier count
- unknown active-agent count
- unknown actual gross margin
- unknown delivery contribution margin
- unknown monthly burn
- unclear legal/regulatory position around the wallet
- no recorded commercial partnership
- no documented customer-learning loop

## What Debridgers Actually Need

### Layer 1: Commercial truth

This is the immediate priority.

I need to know:

1. What customers actually buy.
2. How much they buy.
3. How often they reorder.
4. Why they buy.
5. Why prospects refuse.
6. Which channel produces customers.
7. What delivery really costs.
8. What each order contributes.
9. Whether agents actually sell.
10. Whether farmers can reliably supply.

This is more valuable right now than another 20+ features.

### Layer 2: Density

The model becomes substantially more interesting when there is density.

The proposed flywheel is:

**Farmers -> reliable supply -> attractive prices > agents -> buyer acquisition -> orders -> demand data -> larger procurement -> better supply economics -> more buyers -> higher agent productivity -> more orders.**

Right now, almost every arrow is still hypothetical.

You need to make the first small loop real.

### Layer 3: Working Capital

Once unit economics are proven, capital becomes useful.

But the capital requirement is unusual because your consignment model reduces inventory requirements.

You therefore don't necessarily need:

> "Millions of naira to buy inventory."

You may need:

- operating float
- delivery float
- supplier settlement capacity
- warehouse deposits
- packaging
- customer acquisition
- agent acquisition
- technology
- staff
- eventually purchase-order financing

That is much healthier than an inventory-heavy grocery model.

### Layer 4: Distribution

This is where I think Debridgers has a potentially powerful advantage.

The company should not primarily ask:

> "How do we advertise to Kaduna?"

It should ask:

> **"Who already has 100, 500, 5,000 or 50,000 people who need food?"**

That leads to employers, restaurants, caterers, universities, residential communities, associations, supermarkets and delivery platforms.

### Layer 5: Distribution

Later, Debridgers could become much more than a grocery company.

The potentially valuable long-term asset is:

**regional food supply + demand + price + fulfilment data.**

But don't try to monetize that today.

First generate the transactions that create the dataset.

## Biggest Bottlenecks

### 1. Unit economics - Critical

Right now:

- buyer price: **₦1,400**
- agent remit: **₦1,300**
- visible spread: **₦100**
- handling: **₦100**

Delivery may consume the large proportion for every order.

**This is the first number I would solve**
We now need to build a contribution-margin sheet for **every order**.

For each order:

> - Delivery cost -
> - Refunds/replacement -
> - Promotional discounts
>   = Contribution margin

Until this exists, **we will not optimise GMV**.

### 2. The 5% remittance number

This must be corrected immediately.

The profile says:

> Agent remittance rate = 5%

but elsewhere the intended target is >90%.

And the profile itself correctly notices that 5% literal remittance would be catastrophic.

This is not a cosmetic documentation issue.

An investor seeing:

> "Agent remittance rate: 5%"

will assume the agent network is fundamentally broken.

Determine whether this means:

5% commission
5% outstanding
95% remitted
something else

Then permanently separate these metrics.

Okay then.

### 3. Commercial validation is behind technical validation

This is probably the biggest strategic imbalance in the company.

The engineering work is sophisticated.

The commercial evidence is tiny.

That's a common founder trap:

> Building certainty in software because selling creates uncomfortable uncertainty.

Your next 30 days should therefore probably contain less engineering than you instinctively want.

### 4. Customer segmentation is still too broad

"Households, shops, restaurants, caterers, market traders and institutions" is too many markets for four people.

I would initially concentrate on:

**B2B**

**Restaurants + caterers + food businesses**

because they potentially provide:

larger orders
predictable recurring demand
easier sales measurement
lower orders-to-revenue ratio
stronger procurement data

**B2C**

A tightly defined neighbourhood/household segment around your existing delivery zones.

Do not try to acquire "Kaduna."

Acquire **density**.

### 5. The wallet creates regulatory complexity

This deserves professional review before you scale it.

CBN maintains distinct regulatory categories around payment services, stored value and payment infrastructure, while Paystack and other licensed operators operate within that framework.

The important question is not whether Paystack is licensed.

It is:

> **What legal/regulatory activity is Debridgers itself conducting when it accepts and holds buyer balances and allows withdrawals?**

I would get a Nigerian payments lawyer to answer this before making "wallet" a major part of the product.

The safer commercial architecture may ultimately be to make the financial institution/payment partner the regulated holder of funds while Debridgers remains the commerce layer.

Do not guess here.

### 6. Food compliance

If Debridgers repackages or processes food products under its own operation, regulatory obligations need to be mapped carefully.

NAFDAC's current food regulations address registration, processing/packaging controls and product safety.

The company should determine exactly which activities Debridgers performs:

- aggregation
- storage
- weighing
- repackaging
- labeling
- processing
- distribution

and get a compliance opinion accordingly.

### 7. Data protection

Debridgers holds:

- buyer personal information
- bank information
- agent KYC
- transaction history
- potentially identity documents

The Nigeria Data Protection Act imposes obligations on relevant data controllers/processors, including registration requirements for controllers/processors of major importance.

This needs to become a formal workstream, not a later-stage problem.
