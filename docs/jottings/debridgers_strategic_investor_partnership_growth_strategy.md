# Executive Diagnosis

## The short version

Debridgers has built substantially more technology than it has
commercially validated.

That is not necessarily bad. The technical foundation is unusually
developed for a company with only 10 recorded orders and approximately
₦200,000 of capital on hand. The system already models buyers, agents,
consignment stock, commissions, wallets, payments, order lifecycle, KYC,
outreach and administration, and the money paths have received serious
technical testing.

But the central business question remains unanswered:

> **Can Debridgers repeatedly acquire buyers, source reliable supply,
> fulfil orders at a positive contribution margin, and retain both
> buyers and agents?**

At present, the evidence is insufficient to answer that.

The next 90 days should therefore **not** be dominated by fundraising,
mobile-app development, geographic expansion, elaborate loyalty features
or institutional partnerships for prestige.

The company should concentrate on five things:

1.  **Prove order-level economics.**
2.  **Prove repeatable demand in a tightly defined Kaduna beachhead.**
3.  **Prove agent reliability and positive agent economics.**
4.  **Prove reliable supply and fulfilment.**
5.  **Turn those proofs into an investable data room and a small number
    of high-value strategic relationships.**

The most important strategic shift is:

> **Move from "building the platform" to "building a repeatable
> food-distribution machine."**

# What Debridgers Actually Needs

## 0--3 Months

### 1. Commercial validation

This is the number-one requirement.

Debridgers needs enough transactions to answer:

- Who buys?
- What do they buy?
- How often?
- At what basket size?
- Through which channel?
- Why do they buy?
- Why do they reorder?
- What prevents reordering?
- What does each order cost to fulfil?
- What percentage of orders are profitable?

### 2. A working unit-economics model

Before raising meaningful external capital, Debridgers needs
product-level and order-level economics.

At minimum:

`Revenue per order`

minus:

- true product cost
- agent commission
- payment fees
- delivery cost
- packaging
- warehouse allocation
- refunds/replacements
- promotions/discounts
- customer support
- variable field-sales costs

equals:

`Contribution margin per order`

This must be calculated by:

- product
- order size
- customer segment
- acquisition channel
- delivery zone
- agent/direct channel.

### 3. Agent reliability

The profile says the target is above 90% remittance, but the current "5%
remittance rate" entry is ambiguous and potentially alarming.

Resolve this immediately.

The dashboard should distinguish:

- stock issued
- stock sold
- stock value due
- stock value remitted
- outstanding stock value
- days outstanding
- default rate
- recovery rate
- agent contribution margin
- agent productivity.

### 4. Reliable fulfilment

The system needs to prove the full operational journey:

`Order → payment → confirmation → stock allocation → dispatch → delivery → reconciliation`

The technical fixes already made are valuable, but the live gateway and
real-world operational loop must be tested.

### 5. Real supplier relationships

Debridgers needs a small number of reliable suppliers rather than a huge
farmer database.

The immediate goal should be:

- reliable supply
- known quality
- predictable price
- predictable quantity
- predictable replenishment
- transparent records.

### 6. Local density

The company should dominate a small operating area before expanding.

Current priority zones:

- Sarbon Tasha
- Narayi
- Kakuri

The objective is not "serve all Kaduna."

The objective is:

> **Become the easiest and most trusted way to buy selected staples in a
> small number of dense Kaduna neighbourhoods.**

# Biggest Bottlenecks

## Bottleneck 1 --- Unit economics are unknown

This is the biggest issue.

The current pricing structure creates a potentially thin spread:

- Buyer price: ₦1,400.
- Agent remittance price: ₦1,300.
- Visible spread: ₦100.
- Handling fee: ₦100.
- Additional package delivery fee: ₦500.
- Referral commission: ₦20 per qualifying order, if/when wired.
- Buyer referral discount: ₦500 default.

The company cannot know whether this model works until it knows:

- actual farm-gate cost
- actual warehouse cost
- actual transport cost
- actual delivery cost
- payment fees
- shrinkage
- replacement cost
- commission
- customer acquisition cost.

The technology has been tested heavily.

For the next 90 days, allocate substantially more founder time to:

- sales
- customer interviews
- B2B procurement
- agent recruitment
- supplier relationships
- fulfilment
- unit economics.

- revenue-critical

# Bottleneck 3 --- The remittance metric is ambiguous

The company profile says:

> Agent remittance rate: 5%

but elsewhere the target is:

> above 90%.

This must be corrected.

### Required fix

Define:

```text
Remittance rate =
Value remitted within agreed window
/
Value due within agreed window
```

Track:

- current remittance rate
- 7-day remittance rate
- 30-day remittance rate
- outstanding value
- average days outstanding
- default rate.

Do not use "5%" until its meaning is confirmed.

# Bottleneck 4 --- Regulatory uncertainty around wallets

Debridgers holds buyer balances and supports withdrawals.

This should not be treated casually.

Potential issues include:

- payment services regulation
- custody/holding of customer funds
- KYC
- AML obligations
- consumer protection
- data protection
- payment reconciliation.

### Required fix

Get Nigerian payments counsel to review the exact architecture.

A safer strategic posture may be:

> Let a properly licensed payment institution/bank hold regulated funds
> while Debridgers operates the commerce layer.

Do not assume the current architecture is legally sufficient simply
because Paystack processes payments.

---

# Bottleneck 5 --- Single-city concentration

For now, Kaduna focus is correct.

Expansion is not the answer.

The risk becomes material later because all revenue, supply
relationships and operational knowledge sit in one city.

### Required fix

Do not diversify geographically yet.

Instead define an expansion trigger.

Suggested trigger:

Do not open city two until Kaduna has achieved all of:

- 1,000+ monthly completed orders for at least three consecutive
  months.

- positive contribution margin at the median order size.

- repeat purchase rate of at least 35--40% within the relevant reorder
  window.

- delivery cost within the approved contribution-margin threshold.

- 90% agent remittance.

- at least 20 productive agents or an equivalent direct-sales engine.

- at least 5 reliable B2B accounts.

- no unresolved P0 operational failures.

- sufficient management capacity to operate city one without founder
  dependence.

These are **proposed thresholds**, not facts.

The profile suggests solo or near-solo engineering capacity.

That creates:

- key-person risk
- slower iteration
- operational fragility
- security risk

Do not immediately hire a large engineering team.

5.  Establish incident procedures.

    engineers.

# What Investors Will Like

## 1. The problem is real

Food distribution in Nigeria has obvious structural inefficiencies:

- fragmented supply
- fragmented demand
- price opacity
- logistics friction
- limited market access for farmers
- working-capital constraints.

## 2. Kaduna is a sensible focus market

The company is not trying to launch in Lagos, Abuja, Kano, Kaduna, Port
Harcourt and Ibadan simultaneously.

The density-first approach is strategically healthier.

## 3. Consignment is capital-efficient

The model can potentially grow the agent network without funding every
agent's inventory.

That is attractive if:

- remittance is strong
- shrinkage is low
- agent productivity is high.

## 4. The agent model could become a distribution moat

If Debridgers builds:

- verified agents
- neighbourhood trust
- buyer relationships
- historical sales data
- supplier relationships

then a competitor cannot instantly recreate the network.

## 5. The company is technically serious

The profile documents:

- server-side repricing
- idempotency
- webhook authentication
- database safeguards
- wallet controls
- role-based access
- KYC
- order lifecycle
- automated testing.

That demonstrates engineering discipline.

## 6. The market can expand beyond grocery retail

The B2B opportunity is more interesting to institutional investors than
pure household grocery delivery.

Potential customers include:

- restaurants
- caterers
- hotels
- schools
- hospitals
- offices
- supermarkets
- retailers
- food processors.

## 7. The data opportunity is strategically interesting

If Debridgers eventually observes:

- weekly supplier prices
- buyer prices
- volumes
- product demand
- geographic demand
- seasonality
- agent productivity
- delivery costs

it can build a food-price intelligence layer.

But this is a future moat, not a current one.

# What Investors Will Dislike

## 1. Only 10 orders

This is the clearest commercial weakness.

Ten orders can demonstrate early interest.

They cannot establish:

- product-market fit
- retention
- repeatability
- unit economics
- scalability.

## 2. Unknown revenue and GMV

A serious investor will ask immediately:

> "How much money has actually moved through the business?"

The current answer is not documented.

## 3. Unknown contribution margin

This is the most dangerous omission.

The company cannot responsibly say that the business is profitable,
scalable or capital-efficient until this is measured.

## 4. Unknown CAC

A marketplace/distribution business can look attractive until customer
acquisition costs are included.

## 5. Unknown retention

Repeat purchase is critical.

If a customer orders once because of a promotion and never returns, the
business is structurally different from a customer who buys staples
every week.

## 6. Regulatory uncertainty

The wallet model introduces questions that an ordinary grocery
marketplace might not have.

## 7. Small team

The company appears highly dependent on a small number of people.

## 8. Unclear legal structure

This is a fundraising blocker.

## 9. Unclear competition

The real competitors are not only other apps.

The biggest incumbent is:

> the existing market system.

Customers already have:

- Central Market
- neighbourhood shops
- traders
- informal delivery
- WhatsApp sellers
- personal supplier relationships.

## 10. The "middlemen are the problem" thesis needs refinement

Some intermediaries add real value:

- aggregation
- transport
- storage
- financing
- grading
- risk absorption
- market discovery.

Debridgers should not position every intermediary as economically
useless.

The stronger thesis is:

> **Debridgers replaces opaque, uncoordinated intermediation with
> transparent, measurable and lower-cost distribution.**

That is more defensible.

# Investor Story

## Problem

Nigeria's food system is fragmented.

Farmers often lack reliable direct access to demand.

Households face:

- opaque prices
- travel costs
- haggling
- inconsistent quality.

Businesses face:

- fragmented procurement
- unreliable supply
- price volatility
- repeated supplier negotiations.

---

# Solution

Debridgers creates a coordinated food-distribution layer:

```text
Farmers / Suppliers
        ↓
Warehouse / Aggregation
        ↓
Verified Agents + Digital Ordering
        ↓
Households / Shops / Businesses
        ↓
Transparent Transactions + Data
```

The company does not need to own every truck or farm.

It coordinates the value chain.

---

# Why Now

Several forces support the thesis:

- food inflation and price volatility make price transparency more
  valuable;
- mobile payments and digital commerce make transactions easier to
  coordinate;
- agricultural finance institutions increasingly emphasize data and
  de-risking;
- Nigeria's development ecosystem is actively looking for ways to
  improve food-system financing and youth employment;
- Kaduna provides a focused market in which a local network can
  potentially be built before national expansion.

---

# Traction

The truthful traction story today is:

- 10 orders recorded.
- A functioning technology platform.
- Significant technical testing.
- Consignment model implemented.
- Agent workflow implemented.
- Payment infrastructure integrated.
- B2C and B2B workflows implemented.

Do **not** say:

- "product-market fit"
- "strong traction"
- "large customer base"
- "rapid growth"
- "profitable"
- "validated unit economics"

until evidence exists.

---

# Moat

The moat should not be:

> "We have an app."

Apps are copyable.

The intended moat is:

### 1. Agent density

Trusted agents embedded in neighbourhoods.

### 2. Supply relationships

Reliable farmer/cooperative relationships.

### 3. Demand density

Repeat buyers concentrated in geographic zones.

### 4. Transaction data

Real supply, demand, price and fulfilment data.

### 5. Price intelligence

Historical regional pricing.

### 6. Operational know-how

Route economics, procurement, quality control and remittance systems.

### 7. Financial relationships

Eventually, verified transaction histories can support better financing.

# Strategic Risks and Mitigation

## Agent default

**Risk:** stock disappears or cash is not remitted.

**Mitigation:**

- credit limits
- graduated stock access
- KYC
- references
- transaction history
- automated alerts
- deposit/security mechanisms where legally appropriate.

---

## Fraud

**Risk:** fake transactions, commission abuse, wallet abuse.

**Mitigation:**

- idempotency
- reconciliation
- audit logs
- role separation
- transaction monitoring
- strong authentication.

---

## Price volatility

**Risk:** weekly pricing becomes inaccurate.

**Mitigation:**

- multiple market sources
- price timestamps
- product-level price bands
- transparent methodology.

---

## Supplier concentration

**Risk:** one supplier controls too much supply.

**Mitigation:**

- multiple suppliers
- cooperative network
- geographic diversification.

---

## Delivery cost inflation

**Risk:** delivery destroys margin.

**Mitigation:**

- route density
- scheduled delivery
- zone pricing
- minimum basket sizes
- B2B bulk routes.

---

## Regulatory risk

**Risk:** wallet or food handling model requires additional approvals.

**Mitigation:**

- professional legal review
- licensed partners
- compliance roadmap.

# Final Recommendation

Debridgers should **not** position itself primarily as a grocery startup
seeking investors.

It should position itself as:

> **A technology-enabled food-distribution infrastructure company
> proving a repeatable, transparent and capital-efficient model in
> Kaduna.**

The company's immediate competitive advantage is not the software.

It is the possibility of combining:

- a trusted local agent network
- direct agricultural supply
- recurring B2B demand
- household demand
- efficient delivery
- transparent prices
- transaction data.

The next stage of the company is therefore not "more features."

It is **more transactions with better economics**.

The strategic priority order should be:

```text
1. Unit economics
2. Repeat customers
3. B2B recurring demand
4. Agent productivity/remittance
5. Supplier reliability
6. Delivery economics
7. Compliance
8. Strategic partnerships
9. Non-dilutive capital
10. Institutional equity
11. Geographic expansion
```

If Debridgers proves those first seven, the remaining three become
dramatically easier.
